RIZAL COURSE INTEGRATION PROJECT · ARCHIVAL ZINE

They put him on a one-peso coin so you wouldn’t read what he actually wrote.

School textbooks freeze José Rizal into a safe, polite, marble genius—easy to memorize for an exam, easy to ignore after graduation. That version erases the broke writer shivering through a Berlin winter, the evicted tenant’s son who watched his family lose their home in Calamba, the political strategist who fought bitterly with his own peers in Madrid, and the sharp critic whose diagnosis of Philippine power structures still stings today.

Section 04 · Closing Note

Read the arguments, not just the trivia.

Reading Rizal today isn’t about memorizing his girlfriends, his height, or which European city he visited in 1888. It’s about recognizing how power still operates—how institutions protect themselves, how ordinary people get blamed for systemic failures, and what it costs to refuse being a passive passenger on the Bapor Tabo.
